## 1. Introduction

The K-Pop lightstick, or 
*Eungwonbong* (응원봉), is the quintessential symbol of fan identity, and few are as instantly recognizable as BLACKPINK’s Official Light Stick V.2, affectionately nicknamed the "Ppyongbong" (뿅봉) or the "Hammer Bong." Designed to resemble a toy hammer, the Ppyongbong broke tradition by including a built-in feature: a physical squeaker that activates upon a small impact or press, echoing the toy hammer design.

While the accompanying squeak adds a layer of playful energy during loud, upbeat sections of a concert, it can quickly become problematic. Many BLINKs (BLACKPINK fans) attending shows, particularly those sitting in venues or participating in fan events that require quiet reflection (like during emotional ballad performances or when filming content), seek a reliable method to silence the noisy feature. This guide serves as the definitive manual for managing the Ppyongbong’s unique—and sometimes distracting—auditory signature, allowing fans to maintain perfect concert etiquette while still shining brightly for Jennie, Jisoo, Rosé, and Lisa.

## 2. History and Context

The Ppyongbong Lightstick V.2 was introduced following the group’s initial success, and its design perfectly captures BLACKPINK’s edgy, playful aesthetic. Unlike most contemporary lightsticks that primarily offer visual syncing capabilities (color changes controlled by a central venue system), the Ppyongbong’s distinctive feature is its tactile and auditory feedback. The sound mechanism is not tied to the main electronic board controlling the LEDs or Bluetooth functionality; instead, it is a small, dedicated physical squeaker unit located within the lightstick’s handle or head connection point.

This feature, while endearing to some, posed practical issues. In a crowded stadium, hundreds of unintentional squeaks during a quiet moment can disrupt the atmosphere and irritate nearby audience members. Furthermore, venue staff often request the sound be deactivated in quieter sections or during specific moments requiring reverence. Thus, the quest to "mute the Ppyongbong" became a major topic in online fan communities, leading to several fan-devised solutions aimed at preserving the visual utility of the lightstick while disabling its noise.

## 3. Real-world Examples and Fan Culture: Muting Methods

The need for a quiet Ppyongbong has resulted in shared fan expertise detailing two primary methods for successful sound management.

### Method A: The Non-Invasive Tape Method

This is the preferred temporary solution as it requires no internal modification and is easily reversible.

1.  Locate the Sound Vents: The squeaker noise exits through very small vents, typically located near the handle-to-hammer head connection or along the seams of the handle.
2.  Apply Muting Material: Use a thick, yet temporary, adhesive material like electrical tape, duct tape, or heavy-duty medical tape. Avoid thin clear tape, as it may not fully dampen the sound.
3.  Cover Completely: Press the tape firmly over the identified vents. Ensure the tape creates an airtight seal over the hole.
4.  Test the Squeak: Gently press the hammer head or shake the lightstick. The sound should be heavily muffled or entirely eliminated.

### Method B: The Physical Disablement Method (Use Caution)

This permanent method requires minor modification and should only be undertaken by fans comfortable with delicate dismantling, as it may void any warranty.

1.  Access the Handle: Unscrew and open the battery compartment section, which often provides access to the internal cavity.
2.  Identify the Squeaker Unit: In the V.2 design, the squeaker mechanism is often visible as a small, specialized plastic component connected to the main body, separate from the main circuit board. It looks like a tiny plastic bellows or horn.
3.  Physical Intervention: Gently remove the squeaker unit entirely, or alternatively, locate the small membrane inside the squeaker and carefully puncture or remove it using tweezers or a small screwdriver. Puncturing the membrane prevents air compression necessary for the sound.
4.  Reassemble: Securely reassemble the lightstick. The light and syncing functions should remain intact, but the squeak will be gone.

*Fan Culture Note:* The "Squeak Dilemma" often sparks humorous debates on social media, with some BLINKs arguing the sound is part of the lightstick’s charm, while others advocate fiercely for silence, especially during synchronized light shows where only visual cues matter.

## 5. FAQ

### Q1: Does muting the squeak affect the concert synchronization feature?

A: No. The BL-PING-BONG lightstick V.2 uses Bluetooth technology to sync with the venue’s central control system, changing colors and patterns based on the music. This electronic function is entirely separate from the physical, mechanical squeaker unit. Muting the sound (via tape or physical removal) will not impede the lightstick’s ability to sync or change colors.

### Q2: Is the Ppyongbong sound feature activated only by external pressure?

A: The sound is typically activated by a light pressing or tapping of the hammer head, causing the internal structure to compress the air through the squeaker mechanism. Unlike some electronic sound features, this requires a physical interaction, which is why movement or accidental contact often triggers the sound.
