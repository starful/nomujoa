## 1. Introduction: What is "Bonbangsasu"?

"Bonbangsasu" (본방사수) is a frequently used term in the Korean entertainment industry, especially within K-pop fandoms. It's a combination of Korean words that roughly translates to "live broadcast viewing." More specifically, it's an invitation or call to action, urging fans to watch the *original broadcast* of a TV show or event, rather than a recording or replay. The term is deeply ingrained in K-pop culture, carrying connotations beyond simply watching a program; it signifies support, dedication, and active participation in the success of an artist or group.

For international fans, understanding and participating in "bonbangsasu" can seem daunting initially, given the language and geographical barriers. However, with advancements in technology and the globalization of K-pop, there are increasingly accessible ways for global fans to join in and show their support by watching shows live.

This article delves into the meaning and significance of "bonbangsasu," its history and context within the Korean entertainment landscape, practical ways to watch Korean music shows live from anywhere in the world, and its profound impact on K-pop fandom culture.

## 2. History and Context: The Importance of Live Viewership

The concept of "bonbangsasu" is rooted in the traditional Korean broadcasting system, where viewership ratings directly impacted a show's popularity, advertising revenue, and ultimately, its survival. High ratings translated into increased funding and opportunities for the show's creators and performers. Therefore, encouraging viewers to watch the original broadcast became crucial.

In the context of K-pop, "bonbangsasu" is even more significant. Music show wins are often heavily influenced by factors such as digital sales, physical album sales, social media engagement, and live voting during the broadcast. Live viewership plays a vital role because it contributes to real-time search rankings and, in some cases, direct voting scores. Artists and their management companies actively encourage fans to participate in "bonbangsasu" to maximize their chances of winning.

The emphasis on live viewership is amplified by the fact that Korean music shows are intensely competitive. Numerous artists vie for limited airtime, and only a select few have a realistic shot at winning each week. Securing a win can significantly boost an artist's visibility, leading to more opportunities, increased album sales, and overall career advancement.

The transition from traditional television to online streaming has further shaped the meaning of "bonbangsasu." While the principle remains the same – watching the original broadcast – the methods of viewing have evolved. Fans now have a wider range of options, including official streaming platforms, authorized re-broadcasting channels, and even unofficial live streams hosted by other fans.

## 3. Real-world Examples and Fan Culture: Demonstrating Support

The impact of "bonbangsasu" is evident in the way K-pop fandoms organize and mobilize. Fans often coordinate viewing parties, share streaming links, provide real-time instructions on voting, and troubleshoot technical issues. Social media platforms are flooded with reminders and encouragement to participate in "bonbangsasu."

Here are some examples of how fans demonstrate their support through "bonbangsasu":

*   Streaming Teams: Dedicated groups of fans organize to stream music shows on multiple devices to increase viewership numbers. They often provide guides on how to create accounts, clear cache, and use VPNs to bypass geographical restrictions.
*   Voting Drives: During live broadcasts, many music shows allow viewers to vote for their favorite artists. Fans organize voting drives, sharing tips on how to vote correctly and efficiently. They often use specific hashtags to promote their artist and encourage others to participate.
*   Hashtag Trending: Fans strive to make relevant hashtags related to the music show and their artist trend on social media platforms like Twitter. This increases visibility and generates buzz around the broadcast.
*   Gift Giving: To show support for the artist and the production team, fans often organize gift-giving events, sending food, drinks, and other presents to the set. This is a gesture of appreciation and a way to boost morale.
*   Positive Comments: Leaving positive comments and engaging in discussions about the music show and the performances on social media platforms is another way fans show their support and contribute to the overall online presence of the broadcast.

These activities demonstrate the depth of fan dedication and the significant role that "bonbangsasu" plays in K-pop fandom culture. It’s not just about watching a show; it’s about actively participating in the artist's success and demonstrating unwavering support.

## 5. FAQ: How to Watch Music Shows Live Internationally

Here are some frequently asked questions about how to watch Korean music shows live from outside of Korea:

*   Which music shows offer live streaming options? Some music shows offer live streaming options on their official websites or YouTube channels. Common shows include M Countdown, Inkigayo, Music Bank, and The Show. Check each show’s official website for the most up-to-date information.
*   Are there legal streaming services? Yes, platforms like Viki and Kocowa sometimes offer live streams or near-live broadcasts of Korean music shows with English subtitles. Availability varies by region, so check the specific platform in your country.
*   Do I need a VPN? In some cases, a VPN (Virtual Private Network) may be necessary to bypass geographical restrictions and access live streams. Be sure to research and choose a reputable VPN service. Be aware that using a VPN might violate the terms of service of some streaming platforms.
*   How can I vote during the live broadcast? Voting methods vary by show. Some shows allow voting through their official apps, while others use SMS voting. Check the show's official website for detailed instructions on how to vote. Some voting methods require a Korean phone number.
*   What time do the music shows air? Korean music shows typically air on weekdays and weekends. Check the specific show's schedule for local airtimes and convert them to your time zone. Common shows and times (KST - Korean Standard Time) include:
    *   M Countdown (Mnet): Thursdays at 6 PM
    *   Music Bank (KBS): Fridays at 5 PM
    *   Inkigayo (SBS): Sundays at 3:50 PM
    *   The Show (SBS MTV): Tuesdays at 6 PM
    *   Show Champion (MBC M): Wednesdays at 6 PM
